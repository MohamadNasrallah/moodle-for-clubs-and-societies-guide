Moodle for Clubs and Societies Guide

“Every AUB club in one place, so you can find what’s happening, show up, build something, and be part of it.”

Explore features Open Nexus (opens in a new tab)

Nexus is AUB’s university-wide platform. It runs on Moodle and was previously called Moodle. The Office of Student Affairs (OSA) has rolled out club pages on it for 95+ clubs and societies.

Why Nexus matters

Club pages on Nexus are meant to make clubs easier to find and easier to join in with.

  • 1Attract more students to clubs and societies
  • 2Increase engagement
  • 3Shift clubs from something you only follow to something you take part in
  • 4Make clubs interactive, with signing up, voting, learning, discussing, and building together
  • 5Keep each club’s events, resources, and history in one place so they carry forward from one cabinet to the next

Follow

Something you watch from the outside.

  • See what a club posts
  • Hear about an event
  • Stay a spectator

Take part

Something you are inside of.

  • Sign up and vote
  • Learn and discuss
  • Build together

Get started

Five steps from registering to taking part.

  1. Register for a club

    Use Academic Services > Registration.

  2. Open Nexus and find the club

    Registered clubs appear automatically under My Courses.

  3. Start with the General section

    Get to know the club, its cabinet, and its latest news.

  4. Check Upcoming Events

    See what is coming next.

  5. Join in

    Sign up, vote, discuss, or share feedback.

Inside a club page

Every club page follows the same standard sections, so you always know where to look. Clubs can also add extra sections of their own.

General

Mission, the president’s message, how to connect, the cabinet, and announcements.

Upcoming Events

Flyers, sign-ups, event details, and feedback.

Resources

Useful files, links, and materials.

Archive

Past events, recaps, photos, and recordings.

Governance

Bylaws, handbook, and official club documents.

Extra sections

Optional, such as Learn or Build. Each club decides.

Only the five cabinet positions have editing access to a club page.

See it in action

IEEE AUB is our main example of how a club page can be structured.

Example page structure and ideas

IEEE AUB

These are ideas for how a club page could be set up. They are not a list of what is currently live.

General

Announcements and an optional interest-track choice.

Upcoming Events

Workshop sign-ups, topic voting, and feedback.

Learn

Workshop materials, recordings, and practice questions.

Build

Solve It Tuesday, Tech Weekly, and a member project showcase.

Resources

Materials and useful links.

Archive

Recaps, photos, and recording links.

Solve It Tuesday Idea

A weekly algorithm problem, in any language or pseudocode, with a discussion space and recognition for consistent participation.

Tech Weekly Idea

A Monday forum post with three tech stories summarized in the club’s own words, using IEEE Spectrum as the source.

Ideas for other clubs

These are ideas only. Each club decides what fits.

  • DebateVote on the next motion, then continue the debate in a forum.
  • Photography or artsA shared gallery of member work.
  • Outdoors or sportsTrip sign-ups and a preparation checklist.
  • Cultural clubsA shared glossary and a searchable event archive.
  • Technical clubsWorkshops, practice questions, and project teams.

These ideas are inspired by strong IEEE student branches elsewhere, such as PoliTO recording talks for members who missed them, and Brescia building activity around member projects.

Help

For help with a club page, contact the club’s cabinet or the Student Activities team at OSA.
